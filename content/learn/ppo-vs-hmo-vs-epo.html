Your plan’s network type decides which doctors you can see, whether you need referrals and what happens when you get care outside the network. Two plans with the same premium can feel very different depending on the network. Here’s a plain-English comparison.

The short version

HMOEPOPPO
Out-of-network coverage (non-emergency)Usually noneUsually noneYes, at a higher cost
Need a primary care doctor?Usually yesUsually noNo
Referrals for specialists?Usually yesUsually noNo
Typical premiumOften lowerOften in betweenOften higher

These are typical features; every plan is different, so check the plan’s Summary of Benefits and Coverage. Emergency care is covered at in-network cost-sharing on ACA plans, even at an out-of-network hospital.

HMO (Health Maintenance Organization)

An HMO usually asks you to choose a primary care physician (PCP), who coordinates your care and refers you to specialists. Except in emergencies, care outside the network generally isn’t covered. HMOs often have lower premiums and simpler costs, and work well if your doctors are in the network and you don’t mind the referral step.

EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization)

An EPO is like an HMO without the gatekeeper: you usually don’t need a PCP or referrals, but you still need to stay in-network for non-emergency care. EPOs can be a good middle ground if you want to book specialists directly and your doctors are in the network.

PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)

A PPO gives you the most flexibility. You can see in-network or out-of-network providers without referrals, but out-of-network care costs more. It may have a separate, higher deductible, and the provider can bill you the difference between their charge and what the plan allows (“balance billing”). PPOs usually cost more each month, and they’re worth a look if you travel often, live near a state line or want to keep a specific out-of-network doctor (see travel nurse health insurance).

What about POS plans and HSAs?

A POS (Point of Service) plan blends HMO and PPO features: you typically pick a PCP and need referrals, but some out-of-network care is covered at a higher cost. A Health Savings Account (HSA) isn’t a network type. It’s a tax-advantaged savings account you can open if you have an HSA-qualified high-deductible health plan, which can be an HMO, EPO or PPO.

How to choose

  1. List your must-keep doctors and hospitals and check each one in the plan’s provider directory.
  2. Think about where you get care. If it’s close to home, an HMO or EPO may save money. If you travel or split time between places, look harder at PPOs.
  3. Count how often you see specialists. Frequent specialist care makes referral rules matter more.
  4. Compare total yearly cost: premiums plus what you’d expect to pay for care, up to the out-of-pocket maximum.
