Open Enrollment is the once-a-year window when anyone can sign up for, renew or change an ACA health plan. Missing it can mean waiting months for coverage, unless you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. Here’s what to know.

Key dates for 2027 coverage

In states that use HealthCare.gov, Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs from November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027:

  • Enroll by December 15, 2026 for coverage that starts January 1, 2027.
  • Enroll December 16 – January 15 for coverage that generally starts February 1, 2027.
  • After January 15, you can only enroll or change plans with a Special Enrollment Period.

States that run their own marketplaces can set different dates. Georgia, for example, runs its own marketplace (Georgia Access), and some states extend enrollment to late January. Federal rules on enrollment dates have been the subject of rulemaking and court cases, so always confirm the current year’s deadlines with HealthCare.gov or your state’s marketplace.

If you already have a Marketplace plan

If you don’t act, you’ll usually be automatically re-enrolled in the same plan or a similar one. That’s convenient, but premiums, networks and drug lists can change every year, and so can your tax credit. Before December 15:

  1. Update your application with your expected income and household for next year.
  2. Check that your doctors and prescriptions are still covered.
  3. Compare your renewal with other plans. A different plan may fit better now.

Special Enrollment Periods

Outside Open Enrollment, you can get coverage if you have a qualifying life event, usually within 60 days of it. Common examples:

  • Losing health coverage: leaving a job, losing Medicaid or CHIP, aging off a parent’s plan at 26 (see turning 26), or COBRA running out (see COBRA alternatives)
  • Household changes: getting married, having or adopting a child, or a death in the family
  • Moving to a new ZIP code or county, if you had qualifying coverage before the move
  • Certain income changes that affect eligibility for savings

Voluntarily dropping coverage, or losing it because you didn’t pay premiums, generally doesn’t count. You may be asked to send documents proving the event.

Not everything waits for Open Enrollment

  • Medicaid and CHIP accept applications all year.
  • Employer plans have their own open enrollment, usually in the fall, and a 30-day special enrollment window after events like marriage, birth or losing other coverage.
  • Standalone dental and vision plans can often be bought any time (see dental & vision).

A quick Open Enrollment checklist

  • Your best estimate of next year’s household income
  • Names of your doctors, hospitals and pharmacies
  • A list of prescriptions and doses
  • Social Security numbers and dates of birth for everyone applying
  • Information about any job-based coverage you’re offered
